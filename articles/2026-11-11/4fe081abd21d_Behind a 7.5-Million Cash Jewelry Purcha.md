## I. First Do the Math: What Does 7.5 Million NT Dollars Buy in the International Jewelry Market?

- Exchange rate estimate: 7.5 million NT dollars ≈ 1.7 million RMB ≈ 230,000 US dollars. In the international jewelry market this is a “mid-lot” amount — entry lots at smaller auctions, ordinary lots at the top auction houses.
- Rough conversion (for intuition only):
  - 1 carat, D color, high-clarity round diamond: several hundred thousand RMB, doable but few choices;
  - 3–5 carat colored sapphire (non-top origin): 1.7 million RMB buys decent quality;
  - Top Burmese ruby / Kashmir sapphire: at 1 carat or above this budget often falls short — top quality depends on auction houses and fate.
- More important is the market structure: jewelry is a pyramid — the bottom is brand retail (the biggest markup), the middle is the dealer channel, the top is auction houses and private collection. The same stone can differ severalfold in price across the three layers; that bag of cash is at most a “middle-layer ticket”.

## II. Why Are Gemstones Expensive: Geological Scarcity + Non-Linear Pricing

![img1](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/ryry9966/meyo-assets3@main/images/2026-11-11/9d2a3f0b2518e8eb.png)

- Same origin, different lives: ruby and sapphire are both corundum (Al₂O₃); red ones containing chromium are called ruby, the rest are all sapphires; emerald is beryl colored by chromium/vanadium.
- Formation conditions are extremely苛刻：aluminum-rich， silicon-poor， high-temperature， high-pressure metamorphic environments， hundreds of millions of years of crystal growth， then lifted to the surface by tectonic movements and special magmas. Surviving this “geological lottery” is far harder than digging gold.
- Price doesn't scale linearly with carat: large carats grow exponentially rarer, so a 5-carat top ruby can far exceed 5 times the price of 1 carat; 10-carat-plus top red/blue gemstones may not appear in the public market for years.
- Supply is inelastic: when the gold price rises, mining companies will ramp up; when gemstones are expensive, there's no way to “expand production” — Australia's Argyle pink diamond mine closed in 2020, and traditional sources like Myanmar's Mogok mines keep shrinking. Scarcity is written at the source.
- The pricing formula is roughly: 4C parameters (Carat / Color / Clarity / Cut) + origin premium (Burmese ruby, Kashmir sapphire, Colombian emerald) + certification premium (GIA / GRS / SSEF / Gübelin) + auction records. Each layer stacks, making the final price.

## III. Carrying Cash for a Purchase: Tradition, Privacy, and the Boundaries of Compliance

- Why does high jewelry love cash? Three reasons:
  1. Industry tradition: many jewelry districts historically settled in cash, quick and simple;
  2. Privacy: not wanting a consumption record to become data or marketing material;
  3. Deterministic: wire transfers have confirmation cycles, cash “settles on the spot”.
- But cash isn't a gray-area passport; large cash transactions have clear regulatory boundaries:
  - Taiwan region: precious metals and gemstone retailers handling cash transactions of NT$500,000 or more in a single transaction are designated non-financial businesses under the Money Laundering Control Act, and must confirm the customer's identity and keep records; financial institutions also report cash transactions of NT$500,000 or more;
  - Mainland: banks report personal cash business of RMB 50,000 or more to the anti-money laundering center as large-value transactions; the revised Anti-Money Laundering Law effective in 2025 will also gradually bring precious metals, jewelry, and other high-value goods sectors into anti-money laundering monitoring;
  - Cross-border: international common practice (FATF standards) requires declaration for carrying cash above the US$10,000 equivalent, and customs have corresponding declaration and permit requirements for RMB and foreign currency cash.
